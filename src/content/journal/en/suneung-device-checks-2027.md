Ahead of Korea’s 2027 admissions Suneung (19 November 2026), authorities announced tighter electronic-device checks in response to concerns over smart glasses and miniature cameras. Here are the prohibited-item rules candidates need to know for Suneung and similar exams, along with check procedures for exam teams.

This guide is based on **official announcements checked on 17 November 2026**. For any live exam, follow the latest notices from the Ministry of Education, local education office and exam organizer. Linked official sources are in Korean.

## Prohibited devices in the exam room

Under Ministry of Education rules, prohibited electronic devices include phones, smartwatches, smart glasses and Bluetooth earphones. Only narrow exceptions apply, such as analog watches with hands and no communication, payment or electronic display functions. Anyone who brings a prohibited item by mistake must hand it to a proctor before Period 1 starts; keeping it after that counts as misconduct even if powered off or unused. [Ministry of Education misconduct guidance](https://www.moe.go.kr/sub/infoRenew.do?m=011301&page=011301&s=moe)

Before exam day, check inner bag pockets and coats or trousers for forgotten devices. If you are unsure about the features of your glasses or watch, or require a medical assistive device, confirm the approval process with the organizer in advance.

## Tighter checks on smart glasses

On 17 November, the government announced stronger supervision—including eyewear inspections—to deter cheating with smart glasses and miniature cameras. [Government statement on Suneung misconduct prevention](https://www.korea.kr/briefing/pressReleaseView.do?newsId=156789621)

Because smart glasses can look like ordinary eyewear, staff cannot rely on casual glances, online product photos or familiar brand names alone. Apply the prescribed inspection steps consistently to everyone.

## Pre-exam checklist for operations teams

Schools and exam organizers can use these points when reviewing their own readiness. They do not replace official Suneung proctoring instructions.

- **Current manual:** Confirm every proctor is working from the same dated notice and manual.
- **Escalation path:** Clarify who receives a device report and who makes the call.
- **Permitted gear:** Share on-site verification steps for broadcast/operations equipment and pre-approved medical devices.
- **Factual records:** Set a clear format for timestamps, direct observations, actions taken and staff names.

Record unconfirmed details as unconfirmed so the next reviewer can tell fact from assumption. “New wireless signal received; device not found” and “Candidate seen using a device” are entirely different findings.

## Role and limits of a wireless signal app

Smart Detector is an Android supporting tool that checks wireless signals a phone can receive and logs on-site checks. A signal alone cannot prove that a device is smart glasses, identify its owner or establish cheating—and seeing no signal does not prove no device is present. See [how to read wireless signals from smart glasses and earphones](/en/journal/what-the-app-can-observe/) for details.

**During an actual exam, use only procedures and equipment authorized by the organizer.** Smart Detector is not official Suneung equipment.

## Practicing in school or mock exams

Where permitted—such as school or mock exams—measure the empty room first and watch how the screen changes as people and devices enter. Practicing beforehand makes live readings much easier to interpret.
